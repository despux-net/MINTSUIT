The night smells of vanilla, and of promises that were never meant to age.

I look at the clock: eight o'clock. The same hour the world used to stop at, just to watch us go by. I can still feel the tremor of the waiting — that impatience to come for you with the engine already running, knowing the drive never mattered, because you were always the destination.

You still had those early-millennium eyes, that Y2K spark of neon, mystery, and blind faith in the future. One look between us and the noise outside dissolved, leaving only velvet against skin and the heat of two mouths hungry for something absolute. It was not only desire; it was devotion answered in kind — that rare thing where loving and being loved in equal measure felt as inevitable as breathing.

We were an elegant fire. Flesh, time and fate mixed until it touched the sublime and grazed the divine. Every meeting was a sacred pact between the chaos of the world and the peace of your arms.

Time has passed, but the echo of what we were is still intact. And if I could go back — if the universe granted me a single instant of eternity — I would return to that street, start the car again, and pick you up at eight, just to lose ourselves one more time.
